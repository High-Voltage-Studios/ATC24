PHASE 1 — PRE-FLIGHT: ATIS CHECK PREP
NOTE Before anything — check #atis-information-1 in the ATC24 Discord. Write down the active runway, ATIS letter (e.g. "Information Bravo"), and any NOTAMs. File your /createflightplan command in #flight-plans before calling ground. Leave the squawk field blank — ATC assigns it in your clearance. For military ops, your "arriving" field can be your departure airport (circuit/local ops) or another military base. VFR is standard for local military ops; IFR if transiting to another island.
PHASE 2 — GROUND: IFR/VFR CLEARANCE GROUND
PILOT Garry Ground, [Callsign], [aircraft], information [ATIS letter], request [IFR/VFR] clearance, military operations.
ATC [Callsign], Garry Ground, cleared [IFR/VFR], military operations approved. Expect runway [RWY]. Climb and maintain [alt], expect flight level [XXX] [X] minutes after departure. Squawk [code].
PILOT Cleared military operations, runway [RWY], climb and maintain [alt], squawking [code], [Callsign]. Ready to copy.
NOTE After receiving clearance — update your /createflightplan entry in #flight-plans with the squawk code ATC gave you. Don't push back until that's done.
PHASE 3 — GROUND: PUSH, START & TAXI GROUND
PILOT Garry Ground, [Callsign], request push and start.
ATC [Callsign], push and start approved, face [direction].
PILOT Push and start approved, facing [direction], [Callsign].
PILOT Garry Ground, [Callsign], ready to taxi, runway [RWY].
ATC [Callsign], taxi to holding point [RWY] via [taxiway]. Hold short.
PILOT Taxi to holding point [RWY] via [taxiway], hold short, [Callsign].
NOTE Ground speed limit on taxiways: 25 knots. On the ramp near hangars: 10 knots. These are ATC24 server rules.

PILOT Runway [RWY] cleared for takeoff, [Callsign].

PHASE 8 — FORMATION FLIGHT OPS FORMATION
NOTE Formation flights check in and operate as a single callsign. The lead aircraft handles all ATC comms. Both aircraft use the same squawk code.
PILOT Garry Ground, [Callsign], flight of two [aircraft type], information [ATIS letter], request VFR clearance, military operations.
ATC [Callsign], flight of two approved. [Clearance as normal].
NOTE En route check-in with formation —
PILOT Rockford Center, [Callsign], flight of two [aircraft], level [alt], military operations.
NOTE If your wingman needs to break off formation —
PILOT Rockford Center, [Callsign], wingman is departing the formation, now single ship.
ATC [Callsign], roger, single ship. [Wingman callsign], contact [frequency].
PHASE 9 — VISUAL INVESTIGATION (PROXIMITY OPS) VIS OPS
NOTE ATC24 rules do not permit hard intercepts of civilian traffic. If you want to fly near another aircraft, phrase it as a visual investigation or proximity pass — and only do it if the controller is okay with it and the server is quiet. Never cut off or disrupt a civilian in the pattern.
PILOT Rockford Center, [Callsign], requesting visual investigation of traffic in our area. We have [traffic callsign/type] in sight at [position].
ATC [Callsign], visual investigation approved. Maintain safe separation. Advise complete.
PILOT Visual investigation approved, [Callsign].
NOTE After completing the pass —
PILOT Rockford Center, [Callsign], visual investigation complete. Resuming military operations.

PHASE 13 — EMERGENCY PROCEDURES EMERGENCY
NOTE Update your flight plan squawk to 7700 before transmitting. Controllers are watching squawk codes in ATC24.
PILOT MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY. Rockford Center, [Callsign], [aircraft], declaring emergency. [Nature of emergency]. Requesting immediate vectors to nearest suitable airfield. Squawking 7700.
ATC [Callsign], roger MAYDAY. Fly heading [XXX], descend and maintain [alt]. All traffic, standby.
PILOT Heading [XXX], descending [alt], [Callsign].
NOTE Urgency (non-critical issue) — use PAN PAN instead: "PAN PAN PAN, Rockford Center, [Callsign], [issue], requesting priority handling."
NOTE NORDO (lost comms) — squawk 7600. Fly last assigned heading and altitude. ATC will attempt to light-gun signal or vector you using standard lost-comms procedures.
PHASE 14 — UNCONTROLLED AIRSPACE (UNICOM) UNICOM
NOTE When no ATC is active at an airport, that airport's voice channel becomes UNICOM. You broadcast your intentions for other pilots — no ATC clearance needed, but you still announce your moves.
PILOT Garry UNICOM, [Callsign], [aircraft], taxiing runway [RWY] for departure, military operations, Airbase Garry.
PILOT Garry UNICOM, [Callsign], taking the field runway [RWY], military departure, Airbase Garry.
PILOT Garry UNICOM, [Callsign], [distance] final runway [RWY], full stop, Airbase Garry.
▶ ATC24 SERVER RULES — WHAT YOU MUST KNOW +
  • RULEYou must be verified with Bloxlink and wait 5 days after joining the ATC24 Discord before you can apply to fly on the server.
  • RULEYou must have at least 60 minutes of PTFS flight time on your Roblox account to be accepted.
  • RULEAircraft can only land at airports where they can also spawn. A military jet cannot land at a small civilian airfield that doesn't support it in-game.
  • RULEUse of flares is not permitted on ATC24 — this was specifically noted in the server's application questions.
  • RULEHard intercepts of civilian traffic are not sanctioned. Coordinate proximity ops with the controller before executing.
  • RULESpeed limit below 10,000ft: 250 knots. Military jets may request exceptions from ATC for operational reasons.
  • RULEFile your flight plan in #flight-plans before calling ground. Controllers expect the plan to already be posted.
  • RULELeave the squawk field blank when filing — ATC assigns it in your clearance. Edit the plan to add it afterward.
  • OKYou can fly without ATC clearance when no controller is active — use UNICOM procedures instead.
  • OKMilitary aircraft using VFR for local pattern work or ops area flights is normal and accepted.
▶ MILITARY FLIGHT PLAN TIPS +
  • For local ops (pattern work, low passes, ops area near Garry), depart VFR and list your arrival as Airbase Garry. No destination needed.
  • For cross-island transit to RAF Scampton or McConnell AFB, file IFR with the destination base's ICAO code.
  • Flight level for military jets: FL300–FL450 cruise. Use FL050–FL180 for low-level tactical work. Always state your FL in the plan.
  • Route field is optional — use "GPS DIRECT" or leave blank for direct ops.
  • Your in-game callsign and your ATC24 callsign are separate. The ATC24 callsign (e.g. "Axon 71") is what you use on comms. The in-game name (e.g. "Viper 2020") is just your display name in PTFS.
  • Formation flights: the flight lead files one plan for both aircraft. Wingmen don't file separately.
▶ MILITARY PHRASEOLOGY & RADIO DISCIPLINE +
  • Always end with your callsign. "Climb flight level 350, Axon Seven One." Never leave a transmission unidentified.
  • Read back every clearance exactly — altitude, heading, squawk, runway. Don't summarize or paraphrase ATC instructions.
  • Wilco = will comply. Use when you've already heard the instruction and don't need to repeat it all back.
  • Roger = received only. Doesn't mean you'll comply. Use sparingly.
  • Negative / Affirmative — not "no" or "yes" on frequency.
  • Numbers: FL350 = "Flight level three five zero." Heading 270 = "heading two seven zero." Squawk 4521 = "four five two one."
  • If you miss a clearance: "[Callsign], say again." Short and clean.
  • Don't use the word "intercept" over radio. Use "visual investigation" or "proximity pass" or "military operations in vicinity of [traffic/area]."
  • Keep transmissions short. No filler words. Dead air after you release the PTT is fine.
  • If your intent changes mid-flight, advise center immediately: "[Callsign], amending routing, requesting [new intent]."
▶ BLOCK ALTITUDE & OPS AREA TACTICS +
  • A block altitude gives you a vertical range (e.g. FL250–FL350) to maneuver freely without reporting every altitude change. Request it when you'll be doing hard turns, climbs, and dives in the same area.
  • Request block altitude before entering the ops area, not after you're already maneuvering.
  • Typical military block altitude range: 5,000–10,000ft of vertical spread. Wider than that may get denied depending on traffic.
  • When your ops are complete, always advise center — controllers are holding traffic for your block. Don't leave them hanging.
  • If you're doing low-level work below FL180, advise center of your lowest intended altitude so they can ensure separation from civilian traffic above you.
  • For supersonic runs (SR-71, F-22 at full mil): advise ATC before accelerating past 600 knots and give a general ops area position.
▶ AIRBASE GARRY — FIELD GUIDE +
  • Military-only airbase on Greater Rockford Island. Spawns modern and old military aircraft. No airliners or cargo planes.
  • Short runway — avoid attempting to land very large aircraft (AN-225, B747). P-8 Poseidon fits due to base size.
  • Only fuel tank is on the far side of the ramp, away from the hangars. Plan fuel stops accordingly.
  • The barracks has a canteen, briefing room, and a room for pre-mission RP setup.
  • From the tower you can see Mellor Airport and the Forth Bridge in the distance — useful for visual nav reference.
  • The airbase sits next to the coast and a mountain — expect tight approach angles on certain runway headings. Fly the chart.
  • If ATC is active at Garry, you're in controlled airspace from engine start. If not, use UNICOM procedures.
▶ OTHER MILITARY BASES IN PTFS +
  • RAF Scampton — on Scampton Island off Izolirani. Military jets supposed to land here rather than Izolirani Airport. Connected to South Izolirani by a single bridge. Good for long-range ops from Garry.
  • McConnell AFB — on Cyprus Island. One of four military-only spawn airports. Cross-island transit from Garry makes for a solid long IFR mission.
  • HMS Queen Elizabeth — aircraft carrier. F-35B VTOL and carrier-capable aircraft are the appropriate choice here. Short deck ops only.
  • GR Road Base — small road base on Greater Rockford Island. Suitable for light military or rotary ops.
  • Military aircraft should land at a military base wherever available rather than using civilian airports, per ATC24 realism standards.
▶ SQUAWK CODES ON ATC24 +
  • Leave squawk blank when filing your flight plan. ATC assigns a random 4-digit code (digits 0–7 only) in your IFR/VFR clearance.
  • Edit your flight plan in #flight-plans to add the assigned squawk before pushing back.
  • 7700 — Emergency (MAYDAY / PAN PAN). Update your plan squawk to this immediately when declaring.
  • 7600 — Two-way radio failure (NORDO). Fly last assigned heading and altitude. ATC will work around you.
  • 7500 — Air piracy / hostile acts. Never use this casually on ATC24. It will trigger an immediate server response.
  • Squawk codes on ATC24 are RP only — there's no actual transponder hardware — but controllers still track them via your flight plan for realism and coordination.
▶ ATIS & CHANNEL STRUCTURE IN ATC24 DISCORD +
  • Check #atis-information-1 before every flight. It gives you the active runway, winds, altimeter, and any NOTAMs. Note the information letter — you'll say it when calling ground: "Information Bravo."
  • File your plan in #flight-plans. Format: /createflightplan ingamecallsign: callsign: aircraft: flightrules: departing: arriving: flightlevel:
  • Voice rooms are labeled by position — e.g. IRFD_GND = Rockford Ground, IRFD_TWR = Rockford Tower, IRFD_CTR = Rockford Center. You switch VC rooms when ATC hands you off to the next frequency.
  • When you spawn at Airbase Garry, your ground voice room is the Garry Ground VC.
  • Charts for all airports (ground charts, taxiway diagrams) are in #atc-24-charts.
  • The ATC24 server application requires correct answers to 11 questions — common ones include knowing that the 250kt limit is below 10,000ft, that flares are not permitted, and that an F/A-18 can land at Airbase Garry, McConnell AFB, or RAF Scampton.
▶ HOW ICAO CODES WORK ON ATC24 +
  • ICAO codes are the 4-letter airport identifiers used in flight plans only. On ATC24, you use the full airport name or ICAO in #flight-plans.
  • On the radio, use the full name — say "Airbase Garry" not "IGRY." ICAO codes are filing tools, not verbal callsigns.
  • ICAO codes can be found on the in-game ground charts, the official PTFS charts site, and in the #atc-24-charts Discord channel.
  • Greater Rockford Airport = IRFD. All PTFS codes start with "I" — they are fictional but follow the format of real ICAO codes.
